## 2. Organizational context

- What does the organization do and what information-security outcomes must it achieve?
- Which strategic, financial, technical, regulatory, customer and workforce issues could affect those outcomes?
- Does climate change affect the ISMS or the requirements of interested parties? Record the determination and rationale.
- What material changes occurred in the previous 12 months?

## 4. Leadership and responsibilities

Identify top management, ISMS manager, technical-security owner, people owner, privacy/legal owner, policy approver, risk owners, risk-acceptance authority, control owners, internal auditor and deputies. Record competence and conflicts of interest.

## 5. People and access population

For every worker or service account with an in-scope duty or access, record identity, employment type, start/end date, role, systems, privileges, MFA, device, confidentiality obligation, training and offboarding state. Do not exclude a person while retained access contradicts the declared boundary.

## 6. Technology and data

- Architecture, environments, repositories, identity provider, hosting, database, object storage, analytics, monitoring and AI services.
- Data categories, owners, classification, location, flows, retention, deletion, backups and recovery.
- Production regions, subprocessors and international transfers.
- Authentication, authorization, encryption, secrets, logging, vulnerability, change and incident controls.

## 7. Suppliers and obligations

For every in-scope supplier, record service, owner, purpose, data, region, access, criticality, DPA/contract, SLA, subprocessor list, assurance, incidents, review date and open actions.

Record applicable legal, regulatory and contractual obligations separately. A supplier trust centre is evidence input, not automatic approval.

## 8. Risk method

- Risk identification approach and risk-statement format.
- Likelihood and impact scales.
- Calculation and risk bands.
- Acceptance thresholds and authorities.
- Treatment options, owners, due dates and residual-risk approval.
- Periodic and event-driven reassessment triggers.

## 9. Existing evidence

List approved policies, training records, access reviews, device exports, secure-change samples, vulnerability results, supplier reviews, backup results, restore tests, incident/tabletop records, monitoring results, internal audits, management reviews and corrective actions. Distinguish actual results from blank templates.
